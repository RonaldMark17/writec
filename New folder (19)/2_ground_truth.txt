GROUP 4"
Provincial Government of Aurora vs. Marco GR. No. 202331
DECISION DATE
April 22, 2015

FACTS
Hilario M. Marco (respondent) filed a complaint against the provincial government of
Aurora (petitioner) for monetary claims arising from services rendered.

Marco had entered into a contractual arrangement with the provincial government. However,
the provincial government later refused to honor payment, arguing that the contract was
for lack of proper authorization and compliance with government procurement and auditing
rules.

Marco filed a case to recover the unpaid amounts. The lower courts ruled in his favor,
ordering the provincial government elevated the case to the Supreme Court, questioning
its liability.

ISSUES
1. Whether the provincial Government of Aurora may be held liable to pay Marco
despite alleged irregularities in the contract.
2. Whether the province can invoke non-compliance with government procurement
or auditing rules to avoid payment.

RULING/DECISION
The Supreme Court DENIED the petition and upheld the ruling in favor of Marco.

KEY POINTS OF THE COURT:
Even assuming there were procedural defects or non-compliance with certain government regulations, the
government cannot unjustly enrich itself at the expense of another.

The Court applied the principle of quantum meruit, which allows recovery for the reasonable
values of services rendered when a contract is unenforceable but services were actually performed and accepted.
Since Marco rendered services and the Provincial Government benefited from them, it would be unjust for
the government to refuse payment. Government entities are not allowed to escape liability simply by invoking
technical defects when they have already received and benefited from the services.
